# Why corpus-order tie-breaking inflates MBE raw-text Hit@10

Raw-text scores are integer sums of query weights, so many passages tie at rank 10 (mean tie-group size at rank 10 for MBE raw text: 19 to 45 passages per writer; `raw_cues/tie_sensitivity_summary.csv`, column `tie_size_at10`).

- **Corpus order.** The combined MBE corpus file (training partition, then test partition; 772,001 passages) begins each partition with the benchmark-supplied `mbe_*` records: 2,318 of the 2,600 occupy positions 0 to 2,317, and the other 282 occupy positions 686,324 to 686,605. Every annotated MBE passage is one of these records (`review_diagnostics/revision_numbers.json`, `mbe_unique_reference_sources`). Breaking ties by corpus position therefore favours the annotated passage.
- **Identifier order.** String identifiers sort `mbe_*` after `caselaw_*` and the other source prefixes, so annotated passages fall at ranks 763,818 to 766,417 of 772,001. Breaking ties by identifier therefore works against the annotated passage. MBE graph queries break ties this way (`ORDER BY score DESC, row_index ASC`, `pipeline/prompts/mbe_v1.json`, rule 6).
- **Bounds.** `raw_cues/tie_sensitivity_summary.csv` gives, for each writer, Hit@10 with every boundary tie resolved against the annotated passage (`r_any10_tie_lower`), by identifier (`r_any10_lexicographic`), in expectation under uniform tie-breaking (`r_any10_random_tie_expected`), by corpus position (`r_any10_canonical`) and with every tie in its favour (`r_any10_tie_upper`). For MBE raw text these are, in questions out of 1,071: Qwen 3.8 60 / 65 / 82.6 / 135 / 146; Gemma 4 59 / 67 / 84.1 / 130 / 154; Mistral 42 / 47 / 65.7 / 105 / 117; Terra 76 / 80 / 95.4 / 130 / 138. Corpus order raises Hit@10 1.6 to 2.2 times over identifier order, and uniform tie-breaking raises it by 19 to 40 percent.
- **HousingQA.** Statute identifiers are corpus row numbers and retrieval is restricted to the question's jurisdiction, so annotated statutes are not clustered early in either order; corpus order, identifier order and the uniform expectation agree closely (same file).

The positional facts above were computed from the upstream MBE passage identifiers, which are not redistributed here (see `DATA_ACCESS.md`). The verifier checks every number in the Bounds paragraph against the released tie-sensitivity file.
